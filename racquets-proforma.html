A working tool for racquets directors and GMs

The Programming Pro Forma

Ten private lessons a week at $70, across a 46-week season, is $32,200. Four six-player clinics at $30 a head is $33,120 more. Most finance committees have never seen those numbers on paper. This page turns a weekly racquets schedule into the annual figure a committee can underwrite, and it runs on your club's numbers.

Illustrative Enter the program you would run in year one. Conservative beats impressive. The committee will stress-test whatever you bring, so bring the case that survives it.

Corroborated

The rate card that exists in the wild.

Turnberry Isle in Aventura opened three pickleball and two padel courts in December 2025 with paid, pre-booked access: $40 per 90-minute booking, $15 per hour for guests, lessons from $35 per half hour. Club + Resort Business and the operator's release, December 2025. A committee member who doubts members will pay for court time can be handed a working example instead of an argument.

Champion checklist

Taking the number to committee.

  • Run it twice. Present the conservative case and hold the base case in reserve. A committee that catches one padded number discounts all of them.
  • Staple the demand evidence to it: waitlist counts, clinic fill rates, booking logs. This model prices a schedule. Your utilization data is what proves the schedule fills.
  • Walk the GM through it before the meeting. No GM backs a number they first heard in the room.
  • A board funds a schedule, not a sport. The more of this page that is real bookings and posted rates, the shorter the meeting.
The rest of the case. Programming is the operating leg.
